New annotation schemes, and old schemes in new languages or domains, often start with a prose guideline and little gold data. Producing the first annotations is costly \citep{settles2009active}. LLMs can assist familiar labeling tasks \citep{gilardi2023chatgpt,tan2024annotation}, but whole-structure prompting can violate a scheme's conventions and produce malformed output \citep{ettinger2023expert,sun2024chinese}, and supervised systems need thousands of annotated sentences.

We propose \textbf{decision orchestration} (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}): manually encode a guideline as an executable procedure, separating deterministic dispatch and assembly from local model judgments. Code enumerates candidates, looks up resources and writes the output; models decide what needs understanding, such as a verb's sense or whether a verb and a noun form a light-verb construction. A decision node returns a schema-constrained fragment or distributions over closed options enumerated by code, so LLMs and closed-question services \citep{typesafe2026jev,openai2026decisions,microsoft2026decision} can serve the same procedure. The procedure is then adapted under gold supervision without updating model weights: \textbf{threshold tuning} fits how rules read answer probabilities to the task metric, \textbf{routing} sends uncertain answers to a stronger model, and \textbf{reflection} proposes local edits from aligned traces, which in closed mode are kept only if they measurably help, with one gold document or five gold sentences per round. Every revision is a readable artifact.

We test the method on two schemes that differ in how much of the output is structure, each in Chinese and English: verbal multiword expressions \citep[PARSEME;][]{ramisch2018parseme,ramisch2020parseme}, sets of categorized token groups, and Uniform Meaning Representation \citep[\umr;][]{gysel2021design}, graphs. Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item On PARSEME, a closed procedure learned from \textbf{60 gold sentences}, five at a time, reaches 46.3 F in English, \textbf{4.4 points above the best published result} we know of \citep{rohanian2019bridging} and 13 above the best 2018 shared-task system, both trained on 3,471 sentences, and 57.8 F in Chinese, \textbf{18 points above} Claude Haiku annotating directly with ten gold examples, for under a dollar of reflection per language.
\item On \umr, a 9B model reaches 17.6 F inside the procedure against 4.9 with a detailed prompt, two documents of reflection raise Sonnet from 45.3 to 52.6 F, and routing adds 2.5 F to a closed procedure. But direct generation with ten gold examples matches the best closed procedure in Chinese (42.0 vs.\ 42.6) and exceeds it by 16 F in English: where the output is mostly structure, closed decisions are not enough.
\item Measurement shows where a procedure departs from the annotation: offering all 27 aspect values of the \umr guidelines, where the gold uses seven, together with one dropped polarity rule, cost 5.2 F.
\end{itemize}
