\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compile annotation guidelines into step-by-step procedures and use them to annotate \umr and PARSEME. A fine-grained evaluator traces errors to individual decisions, giving reflection a specific place to revise; this improves 16 of 18 ten-round runs. Without supervised fine-tuning, our reflected procedures surpass the best shared-task system on English PARSEME on the same sentences, and our best \umr scores exceed the published shared-task results on smaller evaluation units. Chinese PARSEME remains below its shared-task best. These results show the potential of building annotators directly from guidelines: decisions and revisions are inspectable, making the procedure easy to debug and improve as more gold annotations arrive. Cheap decision models can carry out the annotation at scale. We see this as a natural first step for future annotation projects, to develop a guideline as an executable procedure and then refine it through fine-grained evaluation as the scheme matures.

\section*{Limitations}
\textbf{Scale.} Test sets are 95--100 sentences per task and language and training is 50 sentences; each chain was run once, with one document order. With 57--59 gold expressions per PARSEME test set, one expression is worth about one point of F, so differences of a few points there should not be over-read; we did not repeat chains with other document orders to measure the variance of reflection. \textbf{Unfinished runs.} OpenRouter limits each OpenAI model to 20 requests a minute for our account, and our API budget was fixed; GPT-6-luna as a \umr fragment decider needs about 3,000 calls per test pass, so we ran it on \umr for two rounds only, tested on 25 sentences, as we did for Sonnet 5.5 as a fragment decider (marked in Table~\ref{tab:main}). OpenAI Decisions was run through OpenAI's own API, the other models through OpenRouter. \textbf{Data editions.} PARSEME 2.0 has no Chinese data and only an English trial set, so we use editions 1.2 (Chinese) and 1.1 (English); the English 1.1 systems predate pretrained models. There is no \umr 3.0 release; we use the 2026 shared-task data, which is based on \umr 2.1, and the shared task's best results are quoted as published, on whole documents, not re-scored on our five-sentence chunks. \textbf{Seeds.} The seeds were written by the authors (with an LLM's help) from the guidelines; their quality affects every result and we have not measured the effort they take. Closed and fragment seeds share the decomposition, so the comparison between modes is not a comparison of decompositions. \textbf{Reflection.} Fragment-mode edits are accepted unmeasured and drift; the same reflector (Claude Sonnet 5.5) was used throughout. \textbf{Models.} All models are commercial services whose behavior may change; we release the predictions, the procedures at every version, and the cached answers that their terms permit.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
We use the \umr shared-task data and PARSEME 1.1 and 1.2 under their licenses (PARSEME 1.2 Chinese: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) and collect no data from people. The method aims to make the first annotations of a new scheme cheaper and easier to check; it could reduce demand for annotation work, and we see it rather as a way to direct annotators to the decisions that need them. Automatic annotations should be checked before they are used as gold. The runs of this version cost about \$59 in logged API charges (decision services \$13, LLM calls \$46), including runs that were stopped; reflection used a subscription. Generative AI assisted the implementation, the running of the experiments and the editing of this paper; the authors are responsible for all content.
