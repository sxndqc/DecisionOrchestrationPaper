New annotation schemes often start with a prose guideline and little gold data. Producing the first annotations is costly \citep{settles2009active}, particularly for Uniform Meaning Representation \citep[\umr;][]{gysel2021design}, which requires sentence graphs and document-level temporal, modal and coreference relations. Its release has only a few hundred gold graphs per major language \citep{bonn2025umr2}. LLMs can assist familiar labeling tasks \citep{gilardi2023chatgpt,tan2024annotation}, but whole-graph prompting can violate semantic-representation conventions and produce malformed output \citep{ettinger2023expert,sun2024chinese}. Our minimal Sonnet prompt reaches 29--33 sentence \F; a 9B model yields no scorable graphs, although a detailed prompt reaches 4.9 \F.

We propose \textbf{decision orchestration} (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}): manually encode a guideline as an executable procedure, separating deterministic dispatch and assembly from local model judgments. For \umr, code controls frame lookup, typed dispatch, coverage and graph assembly; models decide span types, senses, roles and relations. Each decision node returns either a schema-constrained fragment or distributions over closed options enumerated by code. Typed answers affect tree shape and call counts, but cannot invoke arbitrary skills. The same interface supports fragment-producing LLMs and closed-question services \citep{typesafe2026jev,openai2026decisions,microsoft2026decision}.

The procedure is then adapted under gold supervision without updating model weights. \textbf{Threshold tuning} optimizes how rules interpret answer probabilities against the task metric; it does not calibrate those probabilities. \textbf{Routing} replaces uncertain answers with a stronger model's answers. \textbf{Reflection} uses aligned traces to propose local edits. Output provenance helps inspection but is not causal attribution. Closed-mode edits undergo one-at-a-time measurements on training, regression and development documents; fragment-mode edits are unmeasured. Every revision is a readable artifact.

The current evidence is from Chinese \umr. Two fragment-mode reflection documents raise Sonnet from 45.3 to 52.6 \F on T1. Closed-mode runs inherit additional manual seed tuning and development exposure (\S\ref{sec:setup}); threshold adaptation on four documents adds 2.9 \F on H. In a 20-sentence routing pilot, uncertain-question escalation recovers 75\% of the stronger answerer's gain at approximately 26\% of its reported API cost. Training gains exceed test gains, and common-test-set baselines, repeated runs and a second benchmark remain pending.

Our contributions are the manually initialized procedure and interchangeable decision interfaces, adaptation through threshold tuning, routing and supervised edits, and an exploratory study of their behavior in Chinese \umr.

\paragraph{Scope and shared infrastructure.} The companion study \emph{Can LLMs Build Better Annotators? Self-Improving Harnesses for Ontology-Guided Annotation} examines instruction revision in a three-call whole-graph harness. It shares our UMR data, alignment and judge and reuses procedural runs for comparison. Here we study dispatch, assembly and decision interfaces; shared runs are not independent replication.
