\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{\umr data.}
We use the data of the UMR parsing shared task at DMR 2026 \citep{zeman2026umrst}, whose training data is largely based on \umr 2.1 \citep{bonn2025umr2}. For testing we take the shared task's blind test documents for which we have the gold annotation: 13 Chinese documents (\texttt{zh-0021}--\texttt{0024}, \texttt{0027}--\texttt{0035}; 185 sentences) and the four English documents \texttt{en-0005}--\texttt{0008} (95 sentences; \texttt{0005} and \texttt{0007} are two annotations of the same text, kept as in the shared task). Documents are broken up: the Chinese test set is 20 chunks of five consecutive sentences, at least one per document (100 sentences), and the English one all 95 sentences in 20 chunks of at most five. Each chunk is parsed as a document of its own, and its gold keeps the document-level relations between nodes of the chunk. Input is tokenized as in the shared task. For training, reflection reads 10 batches of five consecutive sentences (50 sentences): in Chinese from 10 different training documents (\texttt{0001}--\texttt{0018}), in English from the native-annotation training documents that share no text with the test (the narrative \texttt{0004} and the news documents \texttt{0578}--\texttt{0579}; the training documents \texttt{0001} and \texttt{0003} are excluded, since their text is that of test documents \texttt{0006} and \texttt{0008}).

\paragraph{PARSEME data.}
PARSEME 2.0 \citep{savary2026parseme,scholivet2026parseme} has no Chinese data and only a 16-sentence English trial set, so we use the latest editions with these languages: Chinese from edition 1.2 \citep{ramisch2020parseme} and English from 1.1 \citep{ramisch2018parseme}, both verbal MWEs. Training: 10 batches of five training sentences, three with a gold VMWE and two without. Test: 100 official test sentences, 50 with a VMWE and 50 without, sampled with a fixed seed so that 100 sentences contain enough expressions to score (\zh{中文} 59, English 57 VMWEs). We re-score the shared-task systems' released predictions on exactly these sentences; on the full test files our scorer reproduces their published F (e.g., 71.86 for TRAVIS-mono, 33.27 for Milos).

\paragraph{Systems.}
\textbf{Baseline}: GPT-6.1-sol, a frontier model, annotates each test chunk directly, given only the full guideline (the \umr guidelines, 366k characters; the PARSEME 1.2 guidelines, 335k characters), the sentences (for PARSEME with the lemma, part-of-speech and dependency columns of the input) and the output format; no gold example. \textbf{Seed procedures} are written from the guidelines only (Appendix~\ref{app:seeds}): the instructions, options and tests restate the guideline, the \umr seeds follow the official file format's inventory of relations and attributes, and there is no example anywhere (every \texttt{example.md} of the fragment skills is empty). The fragment and closed seeds share the decomposition and differ in the decision nodes. \textbf{Fragment mode}: decisions by two small, cheap LLMs, GPT-6-luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 (\$0.10 per million input tokens each), with low reasoning effort. \textbf{Closed mode}: decisions by three decision services, Jev 1.13 \citep{typesafe2026jev}, OpenAI's Decisions API with \texttt{gpt-6-luna-decisions} \citep{openai2026decisions} and Microsoft-Decision-1 \citep{microsoft2026decision}, all through the same interface; and the \textbf{cascade}, Jev with uncertain questions re-asked of Haiku 5.5. The \textbf{reflector} is Claude Sonnet 5.5 for every run. Each configuration runs ten rounds of reflection from its seed, and every frozen version $V_0,\dots,V_{10}$ is tested. Within our budget and OpenRouter's limit of 20 requests a minute for OpenAI models, this was done for Haiku, Jev and Microsoft on both tasks and for GPT-6-luna on PARSEME; the OpenAI Decisions chains are reported as far as they ran (Limitations).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\label{sec:metrics}
\umr is scored with the shared task's official \jm{} F1 \citep{zeman2026umrst}: predicted nodes are mapped to gold nodes through their token alignment, and F1 is computed over the triples (concepts, relations, attributes) of mapped nodes, document-level relations included; we also report it without the document level (``no doc''). For diagnosis we use a fine-grained judge (Table~\ref{tab:judge}): it maps nodes with AnCast \citep{sun2024ancast}, breaks the comparison into 25 checks in 11 modules, gives every unit a verdict and every check its own F1, and attributes each predicted unit to the skill call that produced it. PARSEME is scored with the official evaluation script: MWE-based F1 (exact token set and category), token-based F1, and F1 on expressions unseen in the training data. A PARSEME judge sorts every gold and predicted expression into six kinds: correct, wrong category (same tokens), partial (overlapping tokens), missed without a candidate (no rule proposed its tokens), missed after rejection (a candidate had them), and spurious (no overlap with any gold expression).

\paragraph{State of the art.}
For \umr we compare with the best shared-task submission \citep{zeman2026umrst}, entered as a placeholder of 50 F in both languages until it is re-scored on our test chunks. For PARSEME, the best systems of the shared tasks (Chinese 1.2: TRAVIS-mono/-multi, open track; English 1.1: Milos, open track, and TRAPACC, closed track), re-scored on our sentences, and the best published English 1.1 result \citep{rohanian2019bridging}, quoted on the full test set.
