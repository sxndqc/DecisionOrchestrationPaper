\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Tasks}
We evaluate two structured annotation tasks (Figure~\ref{fig:tasks}). \textbf{\umr} maps sentences to token-aligned graphs of concepts, roles and attributes, and adds document-level temporal, modal and coreference relations \citep{gysel2021design,bonn2025umr2}. \textbf{PARSEME} identifies verbal multiword expressions (VMWEs), including discontinuous expressions, and assigns each a category \citep{ramisch2018parseme,ramisch2020parseme}.

\paragraph{Data}
For \umr, we use Chinese and English data from the DMR 2026 shared task, largely based on \umr 2.1 \citep{zeman2026umrst}. We select 50 training sentences per language in ten batches of five; the test sets contain 100 Chinese and 95 English sentences. We use shared-task tokenization. For PARSEME, we use Chinese edition 1.2 \citep{ramisch2020parseme} and English edition 1.1 \citep{ramisch2018parseme}. Each language has 50 training sentences in ten batches of five (three with a VMWE and two without per batch) and 100 sampled official test sentences (50 with a VMWE and 50 without).
Table~\ref{tab:skills} lists the decision skills and rule nodes, Figure~\ref{fig:walk} shows a \umr trace, and Appendix~\ref{app:seeds} gives the seed instructions.

\paragraph{Baseline}
We use GPT-6.1-sol to generate the annotation in a single call per test chunk, given the full guideline, the input sentences (including lemma, part-of-speech and dependency columns for PARSEME) and the required output format. The guideline itself contains examples; we provide no separate gold examples.

\paragraph{Seeding}
We write the seed procedures solely from the published guidelines before reading any training gold. Neither mode includes worked examples (Appendix~\ref{app:seeds}). In fragment mode, decision skills restate the relevant guideline in natural language and return schema-constrained graph fragments or expressions. In closed mode, we encode possible outputs as fixed-choice questions, with guideline tests, thresholds and deterministic branches; the model supplies choices and code assembles the annotation.

\paragraph{Models and batches}
Each training batch contains five sentences. Fragment-mode parsing uses GPT-6-luna and Claude Haiku 5.5; the smaller comparison also uses Claude Sonnet 5.5. Closed-mode parsing uses Jev 1.13 \citep{typesafe2026jev}, OpenAI Decisions with \texttt{gpt-6-luna-decisions} \citep{openai2026decisions}, and Microsoft-Decision-1 \citep{microsoft2026decision}. Claude Sonnet 5.5 performs reflection.

\paragraph{Reflection protocol.}
\label{sec:reflection-protocol}
After each training batch, the reflector compares predictions with gold and proposes edits. In closed mode, an edit is kept if it improves the current batch without lowering earlier batches by more than 0.5 F, or improves earlier batches without hurting the current batch. In fragment mode, valid edits are kept and evaluated in the next round (Algorithm~\ref{alg:reflect}).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\label{sec:metrics}
We report official \jm{} F1 for \umr, with and without document-level relations \citep{zeman2026umrst}, and official MWE-based F1 for PARSEME, requiring an exact token set and category match. Secondary analyses use a fine-grained \umr judge (Table~\ref{tab:judge}) and PARSEME token-based and unseen-expression F1.

\paragraph{State of the art.}
For PARSEME, we re-score TRAVIS-multi (Chinese 1.2) and Milos (English 1.1) on our test sentences. For \umr, we report Orange/08's published full-test scores \citep{zeman2026umrst}; its outputs are unavailable for re-scoring on our chunks.
