\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Tasks.}
We evaluate two structured annotation tasks (Figure~\ref{fig:tasks}). \textbf{\umr} maps sentences to token-aligned graphs of concepts, roles and attributes, and adds document-level temporal, modal and coreference relations \citep{gysel2021design,bonn2025umr2}. \textbf{PARSEME} identifies verbal multiword expressions (VMWEs), including discontinuous expressions, and assigns each a category \citep{ramisch2018parseme,ramisch2020parseme}.

\paragraph{Data.}
For \umr, we use Chinese and English data from the DMR 2026 shared task, largely based on \umr 2.1 \citep{zeman2026umrst}. Each language has 50 training sentences in ten batches of five; the test sets contain 100 Chinese and 95 English sentences. We parse test chunks of at most five consecutive sentences independently, using shared-task tokenization. For PARSEME, we use Chinese edition 1.2 \citep{ramisch2020parseme} and English edition 1.1 \citep{ramisch2018parseme}. Each language has 50 training sentences in ten batches of five (three with a VMWE and two without per batch) and 100 sampled official test sentences (50 with a VMWE and 50 without).
Table~\ref{tab:skills} lists the decision skills and rule nodes, Figure~\ref{fig:walk} shows a \umr trace, and Appendix~\ref{app:seeds} gives the seed instructions.

\paragraph{Baseline.}
GPT-6.1-sol generates the annotation in a single call per test chunk, given the full task guideline, the input sentences (including lemma, part-of-speech and dependency columns for PARSEME) and the required output format, with no gold examples.

\paragraph{Seeding.}
We write the seed procedures by hand from the published guidelines before reading any training gold; neither mode includes worked examples (Appendix~\ref{app:seeds}). Both modes use the same task decomposition and rule nodes. In fragment mode, decision skills restate the relevant guideline in natural language and return schema-constrained graph fragments or expressions. In closed mode, we encode the guideline's tests as fixed-choice questions, thresholds and deterministic branches; the model supplies choices and code assembles the annotation. The \umr seeds follow the official file format's inventory of relations and attributes.

\paragraph{Models and runs.}
Fragment decisions use GPT-6-luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 (\$0.10 per million input tokens each), with low reasoning effort. Closed decisions use Jev 1.13 \citep{typesafe2026jev}, OpenAI's Decisions API with \texttt{gpt-6-luna-decisions} \citep{openai2026decisions} and Microsoft-Decision-1 \citep{microsoft2026decision}, all through the same interface; the cascade re-asks uncertain Jev questions of Haiku 5.5. Claude Sonnet 5.5 reflects in every run. Each configuration runs ten rounds of reflection from its seed, and every frozen version $V_0,\dots,V_{10}$ is tested. Within our budget and OpenRouter's limit of 20 requests a minute for OpenAI models, this was done for Haiku, Jev and Microsoft on both tasks and for GPT-6-luna on PARSEME; the OpenAI Decisions chains are reported as far as they ran (Limitations).

\paragraph{Reflection protocol.}
\label{sec:reflection-protocol}
Each round parses one batch of five gold sentences. The reflector reads gold and predicted annotations, unit-level scores, attributed errors, decision traces and editable files, then proposes additions, replacements or deletions (Algorithm~\ref{alg:reflect}). In closed mode, each edit is applied to a copy and evaluated on the current and all earlier training batches. It is kept if current-batch F improves and earlier-batch F falls by at most 0.5, or earlier-batch F improves without a current-batch loss. Rejected edits are returned once with their measured effects. In fragment mode, edits are accepted if they apply and leave the procedure loadable, then tested in the next round.

Files exceeding $1.5\times$ their seed length are compressed, merging redundant rules while preserving options, questions and conventions. In closed mode, a compressed version is kept only if neither batch set loses more than 0.5 F. Each round freezes the revised procedure with a reviewable diff. Edits copying six consecutive Chinese characters or four English words from test text are rejected.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\label{sec:metrics}
\umr is scored with the shared task's official \jm{} F1 \citep{zeman2026umrst}: predicted nodes are mapped to gold nodes through their token alignment, and F1 is computed over the triples (concepts, relations, attributes) of mapped nodes, document-level relations included; we also report it without the document level (``no doc''). For diagnosis we use a fine-grained judge (Table~\ref{tab:judge}): it maps nodes with AnCast \citep{sun2024ancast}, breaks the comparison into 25 checks in 11 modules, gives every unit a verdict and every check its own F1, and attributes each predicted unit to the skill call that produced it. PARSEME is scored with the official evaluation script: MWE-based F1 (exact token set and category), token-based F1, and F1 on expressions unseen in the training data. A PARSEME judge sorts every gold and predicted expression into six kinds: correct, wrong category (same tokens), partial (overlapping tokens), missed without a candidate (no rule proposed its tokens), missed after rejection (a candidate had them), and spurious (no overlap with any gold expression).

\paragraph{State of the art.}
For \umr we compare with the best shared-task submission \citep{zeman2026umrst}, entered as a placeholder of 50 F in both languages until it is re-scored on our test chunks. For PARSEME, the best systems of the shared tasks (Chinese 1.2: TRAVIS-mono/-multi, open track; English 1.1: Milos, open track, and TRAPACC, closed track), re-scored on our sentences, and the best published English 1.1 result \citep{rohanian2019bridging}, quoted on the full test set.
