\begin{abstract}
Annotating a complex ontology needs both an understanding of the structure it describes and the scheme's codebook. Early in a project, when gold data is scarce, large language models can bootstrap the annotation, but a guideline of hundreds of pages is too long to follow in context, and when a model writes the whole annotation at once, its errors cannot be traced to the rule it misapplied. We propose \emph{decision orchestration}: the guideline is compiled into a procedure of small steps in which code cuts, enumerates and assembles, and models are consulted only at decision nodes, either writing a small schema-checked fragment or answering closed questions with probabilities that rules read against thresholds. Every output unit comes from a logged step, so every error has an address, and a reflector LLM uses these addresses to edit the procedure from five gold sentences at a time. On samples of Uniform Meaning Representation (the 2026 shared task) and PARSEME verbal multiword expressions, in Chinese and English, seed procedures written from the guidelines alone, with no examples and decided by small LLMs or by decision models that never write text, match or beat a frontier model given the whole guideline (Chinese \umr: 33.9 against 23.9). Fifty gold sentences of reflection improve eleven of twelve procedures, by up to 31 points; the best \umr procedure (Chinese, 37.6) is decided by a model that costs under 30 cents per hundred sentences, and on English PARSEME every reflected procedure exceeds the best shared-task system on the same sentences. The procedures stay readable and diagnosable throughout, which makes them a practical first step for new annotation projects.
\end{abstract}
