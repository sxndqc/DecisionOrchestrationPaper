\begin{abstract}
A new annotation scheme may have a guideline but little gold data. Whole-graph prompting can violate conventions and produce malformed output, while supervised parsing often requires substantially more labeled examples. We observe that a guideline is mostly a procedure: most steps are deterministic, and only a few call for judgment. We propose \emph{decision orchestration}, which manually encodes a guideline as an executable procedure separating rule nodes from model decision nodes. Code runs the steps, assembles and validates the output, and a model is consulted only at decision points, for a schema-constrained fragment or for probabilities over the options of a closed question. We then learn the procedure itself, not model weights, from a handful of gold documents. Decision thresholds are optimized against the task metric, uncertain decisions are routed to a stronger model, and reflection uses aligned traces to localize discrepancies. In closed mode, proposed edits are retained through measured trials; fragment-mode edits are unmeasured. On Chinese Uniform Meaning Representation parsing, the 9B fragment procedure scores 17.6 F against 0 for a minimal prompt and 4.9 for a detailed prompt, two gold documents raise a frontier model from 45.3 to 52.6 F, recalibration on four documents adapts a new decision model (+2.9 F), and routing recovers 75\% of a stronger model's gain at approximately 26\% of its reported API cost on a 20-sentence pilot. Evidence is limited to Chinese UMR; common-test-set baselines, repeated runs and the proposed second benchmark remain pending.
\end{abstract}
