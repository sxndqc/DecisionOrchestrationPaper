Table~\ref{tab:main} compares each procedure with one-shot generation baseline from GPT-6.1-sol given the full guideline. Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows every iteration's performance on test data; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:all} gives the scores. Reflection improves 16 of the 18 ten-round chains. The largest gains are in closed PARSEME: Jev rises from 37.8 to 63.4 in Chinese and 22.5 to 53.7 in English; OpenAI Decisions rises from 35.1 to 54.5 and 42.0 to 56.6. On \umr, Jev reaches the best Chinese score (37.6), while Haiku reaches the best English score on the full test sample (32.6). Sonnet 5.5 reaches 75.9 on Chinese PARSEME after three rounds and the highest English \umr score, indicating a stronger LLM indeed performs better.

\paragraph{How Improvement Happens}
In the first Chinese \umr batch, Haiku's fragment procedure produced English concept names for Chinese words. Reflection added the convention that Chinese event and entity concepts retain the source word to the concept decisions; across that round, test \jm{} F1 rose from 20.9 to 27.8. By $V_{10}$, its event-concept check rises from 30.0 to 77.3 and its entity-concept check from 36.3 to 73.8 (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-umr}). The PARSEME reflector likewise sharpened the closed light-verb test, including the rule that \zht{有} is not a light verb. Across Jev's ten Chinese rounds, spurious predictions fall from 80 to 9, correctly categorized expressions rise from 7 to 22, and test F1 rises from 37.8 to 63.4 (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-parseme}). These are examples of revised decision criteria; the gains across rounds include other edits.

The reflector lists 9--11 problems per \umr batch and 2--4 per PARSEME batch, and proposes about one edit per problem (Table~\ref{tab:rsi}). About half the closed-mode edits survive measurement. Fragment edits are kept when they apply, without a score check: Haiku's Chinese PARSEME peaks at 62.9 in round 3 but ends at 53.2. Retained edits include \texttt{exist-91} for \zh{X中有Y}, the rule that speech content is not discourse, and an inceptive-aspect rule.

% The \umr judge (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-umr}) shows gains in 29 of 32 Chinese checks and 28 of 32 English checks. 
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-umr} shows that at $V_{10}$, closed decisions do better where code constrains the structure: Chinese node granularity is 92.9 vs.\ 85.8 for fragments, and person and number is 49.0 vs.\ 23.2. Fragments do better on open judgments: event arguments are 45.6 vs.\ 35.7 and coreference 16.4 vs.\ 12.7. The gains are uneven: Haiku's Chinese core arguments rise by 23.4 points while Jev's fall by 2.1; Jev's English event concepts lag Haiku's (55.6 vs.\ 72.5). The trace attributes 51 of Jev's 212 wrong English concepts to inflected forms such as \textit{stolen} and \textit{thought} that its rule-based lemmatizer fails to reduce.

On PARSEME (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-parseme}), some closed seeds over-accept candidates: Jev and Microsoft make 78--123 spurious predictions against 57--59 gold expressions. Reflection reduces spurious predictions in all six closed chains (Jev: 80 to 9 Chinese, 112 to 19 English; Microsoft: 123 to 11 English). It can also reject good candidates: Microsoft's English correct predictions fall from 17 to 10 and missed gold expressions rise from 12 to 32, although its F1 improves from 25.9 to 43.5. Closed procedures additionally miss 8--18 expressions at $V_{10}$ because no rule proposes their tokens, against 3--7 for fragments, which may return expressions outside the candidate list.

Table~\ref{tab:calib} compares each closed procedure with its thresholds as written and after calibration on the 50 training sentences. Calibration helps six of eight closed seeds but changes reflected \umr procedures by at most 0.2 points and can hurt PARSEME (Jev Chinese: 63.4 to 61.4). Reflection has already adjusted the decisions, and refitting thresholds on so little data can overfit. The learned procedure also transfers between deciders (last row of Table~\ref{tab:calib}): Jev's $V_{10}$, answered by Microsoft-Decision-1, beats Microsoft's own $V_{10}$ in three of four cases (Chinese PARSEME: 57.1 vs.\ 46.0, or 62.0 after calibration). Cascading uncertain decisions to a small LLM is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:cascade}.
