\paragraph{Steps beat the guideline in context.}
Without any gold, the closed seeds are 9--11 points above GPT-6.1-sol on Chinese \umr (33.2--35.0 against 23.9), and within three points of it on English \umr. On Chinese PARSEME the fragment seeds start well above it (59.5--72.1 against 50.0); on English PARSEME, a scheme close to what frontier models know, every seed stays below it (75.6).

\paragraph{Fifty gold sentences of reflection.}
Reflection improves sixteen of the eighteen ten-round chains. The largest gains are on PARSEME with measured closed edits: Jev 37.8 $\to$ 63.4 (Chinese) and 22.5 $\to$ 53.7 (English), OpenAI Decisions 35.1 $\to$ 54.5 and 42.0 $\to$ 56.6. On \umr, Jev reaches 37.6 in Chinese, the best \umr procedure, and Haiku 32.6 in English; Haiku's Chinese seed gains 6.9 points in round 1 alone, by learning that Chinese concepts keep the source word. Unmeasured fragment edits are less stable: Haiku's Chinese PARSEME procedure peaks at 62.9 ($V_3$) and ends at 53.2. A larger fragment decider, Sonnet 5.5, gives the best Chinese PARSEME procedure (75.9 after three rounds) and the best English \umr scores on its 25-sentence test.

\paragraph{Against the state of the art.}
On English PARSEME every reflected procedure is above the best 1.1 system on the same sentences (Milos, 40.5), though not above the frontier model in context. On Chinese PARSEME the 1.2 systems, trained on the full data, stay ahead (81.1 against 75.9). On \umr our best procedures (37.6 Chinese, 32.6 English) score above the best shared-task submission's published results (36.5, 22.2), on smaller evaluation units (five-sentence chunks against whole documents), so the comparison is indicative. The comparison with the frontier model also depends on the metric: AnCast++, which matches nodes by content instead of token alignment, puts GPT-6.1-sol first (44.8 Chinese, 45.8 English, against 42.2 and 37.5 for Haiku's $V_{10}$). Our procedures get their alignments from code, so their advantage on \umr holds on the official metric, not on graph content alone.

\subsection{Where fragment and closed decisions differ}
\label{sec:case}


Because every unit is attributed to the step that made it, reflection's gains and the two modes can be compared check by check (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-umr}). Reflection raises 29 of the 32 Chinese scores and 28 of the 32 English ones, most of all where the seed was weakest (Haiku's Chinese event concepts 30.0 $\to$ 77.3, entity concepts 36.3 $\to$ 73.8, core arguments 22.2 $\to$ 45.6). At $V_{10}$, closed decisions win where code controls the output (node granularity, Chinese 92.9 against 85.8; person and number, 49.0 against 23.2), fragment decisions where judgment is open (event arguments, 45.6 against 35.7; coreference, 16.4 against 12.7). In English, Jev loses on event concepts (55.6 against 72.5), and the trace shows why: 51 of its 212 wrong concepts are inflected forms (\textit{stolen}, \textit{thought}) that the seed's rule-based lemmatizer did not reduce. A decision model can only pick among the options code offers, so the closed mode is as good as its enumerator; such an error is found in one column of the judge and is cheap to fix (an irregular-verb table).

On PARSEME (Figure~\ref{fig:judge-parseme}), the closed seeds start low because they accept far too many candidates (78--123 spurious predictions for 57--59 gold expressions). Reflection teaches them the scheme's sense of ``light'' and ``idiomatic'' (``\zht{有} is not a light verb''), spurious predictions fall in all six closed chains (Jev: 80 $\to$ 9 in Chinese, 112 $\to$ 19 in English; Microsoft, English: 123 $\to$ 11), and correct ones rise in five of the six. What closed procedures cannot recover are expressions no rule proposes (11--16 missed, against 3--7 for fragment procedures).

Threshold calibration (\S\ref{sec:calibration}) helps six of the eight seeds but leaves the reflected procedures unchanged or worse (Table~\ref{tab:calib}; within $\pm0.2$ on \umr, Jev Chinese PARSEME 63.4 $\to$ 61.4): measured reflection has already tuned the thresholds, and refitting them on 50 sentences overfits. \subsection{What reflection does}
\label{sec:res-rsi}

\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.6pt}

The reflector lists 9--11 problems per \umr batch and 2--4 per PARSEME batch and proposes about one edit per problem (Table~\ref{tab:rsi}). Measurement keeps about half of the closed-mode edits; fragment mode keeps every edit that applies, so its procedures grow faster (Chinese \umr 36.8k $\to$ 50.8k characters, against 70.5k $\to$ 73.7k in closed mode). The kept edits read like a manual's errata (``\texttt{exist-91} for \zh{X中有Y}'', ``speech content is not discourse'', ``add inceptive aspect''), each a readable change to a named step that an annotator can check, keep or revert.
