\section{Methodology: Decision Orchestration}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Workflow and components}
A registry runs rule nodes and decision nodes to produce an annotation and an execution trace. On gold data, a judge scores individual annotation units, and a reflector uses the scores and trace to revise the procedure. We represent this system as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:procedure}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi &= (\mathcal{S}, R, C, M, W, \Theta, J, \mathcal{F}),\\
s_i &= (I_i,O_i,d_i,T_i)\in\mathcal{S},\\
\mathcal{F} &= (F_{\mathrm{diag}},F_{\mathrm{valid}},F_{\mathrm{comp}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{S}$ contains decision nodes $s_i$, each implemented as a skill with input and output schemas $I_i,O_i$, instructions $d_i$, and available tools $T_i$. $R$ is the registry, $C$ the deterministic code, $W$ the worktree, $M$ the annotation and reflection models, and $\Theta$ numerical parameters such as thresholds. $J$ is the set of fine-grained judges; $\mathcal{F}$ comprises diagnosis, edit validation and compression.

$R$ executes nodes in ordered groups (\emph{waves}), dispatching a rule node in $C$ or a decision node in $\mathcal{S}$(\emph{emits}), categorizes outputs to worktree $W$ or to next nodes, and assembles the final outputs from the worktree $W$. At $s_i$, $M$ evaluates the input under $d_i$, with supporting information from $T_i$; $C$ validates or converts its answer to an emit conforming to $O_i$, using $\Theta$ for closed decisions, and assembles the annotation. $J$ scores it against gold in fine-grained aspects, along with a \textit{comprehensive score} defined by the annotation benchmark; $\mathcal{F}$ invokes a reflector in $M$ to locate errors, propose edits to the responsible instructions or code, then validates and compresses the revised procedure to avoid skill instructions from becoming too verbose.

\paragraph{Modes of Decision nodes}
\label{sec:modes}
Decision nodes have two implementations with the same emit.
In \textbf{fragment mode}, an LLM reads the skill's instructions and input, uses its available tools, and returns a graph fragment in structured outputs.
In \textbf{closed mode}, the system uses codes to enumerate options and asks choice, yes/no or graded questions. A decision model returns probabilities over these options, which rules convert into the emit using thresholds in $\Theta$. 
Both modes log inputs and outputs; closed mode also records options and probabilities.

\paragraph{Cascading}
For closed decisions with confidence lower than a \textit{cascading threshold}, a \textbf{cascading} mechanism routes the input to a stronger model that receives the same inputs and replaces the original answer.

\paragraph{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}
For the \textit{cascading threshold} above, and to account for different decision models' output probabilities for\textit{decision thresholds}, we fit both in 0.1 increments on a small batch of training annoations for a best comprehensive score.

\paragraph{Fine-grained evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
The judges in $J$ score individual annotation units against gold; execution traces link predictions to the responsible steps, providing targeted feedback for reflection. Task-specific checks and scoring details are given in the Metrics paragraph of Experiments (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).

\paragraph{Reflection}
\label{sec:reflect}
Reflection revises the procedure in three steps:
\textbf{Diagnose ($F_{\mathrm{diag}}$):} use judge feedback and execution traces to locate errors and propose targeted edits.
\textbf{Validate ($F_{\mathrm{valid}}$):} check candidate revisions and retain those that satisfy the acceptance criteria.
\textbf{Compress ($F_{\mathrm{comp}}$):} merge redundant instructions and rules while preserving required options and conventions.
The experimental protocol is detailed in \S\ref{sec:reflection-protocol} (Reflection protocol).
