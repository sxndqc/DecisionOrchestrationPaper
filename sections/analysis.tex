\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Traces localize discrepancies.}
The trace records the producer of each predicted unit, while gold-only missing units remain unassigned. This supports inspection of candidate omissions and error propagation, but a producer is not necessarily the causal source or best repair target. Fine-grained judge counts and scores in Table~\ref{tab:lost} require recomputation against the current gold revision; their cells are left blank rather than interpreted as current evidence \exper{E13}.

\paragraph{Replacing an answerer under fixed questions and rules.}
On 0010 and 0012 with $J_2$'s questions and rules, replacing Jev answers with Sonnet raises \F from 39.1 to 43.5. This paired pilot measures the answerer's effect under those questions and rules. It cannot locate the remaining bottleneck by comparison with the fragment seed's 45.3 on T1, a different set of documents. Candidate coverage, rules and tree shape remain possible constraints, to be tested with same-document interventions and oracle answers \exper{E6}. Among the 29 accepted measured edits, 22 change assembly rules, four option sets, one adds a tool, one a question and one changes wording; these counts describe what the optimizer changed, not the causal share of each component in the gains.

\paragraph{Measurement filters proposed edits.}
Unmeasured reflection is not filtered. In fragment mode each round finds 376--902 problems (29--43\% at the document level) and applies 35--73 edits, and only 1 of 441 edits is rejected. The six skills grow from 41k to 156k characters over ten rounds, the same accumulation of rules that makes hand-maintained guidelines hard to use. The following closed-mode trial diagnostics are historical log summaries whose original run artifacts and scorer versions remain to be checked \exper{E13}. With measurement, 19 of 48 proposals in four rounds were reverted, among them third-round attempts to \emph{undo} earlier fixes, which the regression documents rejected. A retry with feedback often rescues an edit; one edit that first dropped a document by 2.95 \F was rewritten into one that raised the objective by 1.43 with no document falling.

\paragraph{Training gains exceed observed test gains.}
The measured-reflection trajectory has larger improvements on documents used for diagnosis and selection than on H. Round 4 measured three development documents and improved their mean by 0.8 \F, yet the H mean declined from 40.9 to 40.1. Limited cold reruns are insufficient to rule out stochastic variation. Document-specific conventions, restricted development coverage, adaptive selection and answerer behavior are possible explanations; the current trajectory does not separate them. We show the last reported round and intermediate trajectory; this does not establish a preregistered checkpoint-selection policy. Choosing a version using H would require a new untouched final test set. Repeated trajectories and matched acceptance-policy ablations remain pending \exper{E3}, \exper{E6}, \exper{E8}.

\paragraph{Weak reflectors and cost.}
With Qwen3.5-9B as parser and reflector, each new document parsed with the previous version is 1.4 \F \emph{below} the seed on average (noise: 2.3). The 9B reflector rewrites the anchors it should copy (1 of 15 edits applied) and writes noisy rules, so attribution alone is not enough. As for cost, fragment mode with Sonnet takes dozens of calls per sentence (\$0.71 per sentence on T1), whereas closed mode took 7,552 requests and about \$0.64 for 216 sentences, and four rounds of measured reflection about \$8 in decision requests plus 36--82 reflector calls per round \exper{E11}.
