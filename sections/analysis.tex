\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Where closed decisions lose on \umr.}
The answerer matters (with $J_2$'s questions fixed, Sonnet's answers raise \F on 0010+0012 from 39.1 to 43.5), but not enough. We replaced parts of an earlier run of $K_4$ on H (40.4 \F) by gold, using the scorer's alignment of predicted to gold nodes. Making every concept form, aspect, role label and attribute of the aligned nodes correct raises \F to 49.0; also making the arguments among aligned nodes correct raises it to 55.9. The rest of the gap is nodes the procedure never built. Correct aspect alone is worth 2.9 \F, but combining Jev with a lexical prior from the training gold gives 0.68--0.716 aspect accuracy on H, against 0.714 for Jev alone. Attachment is the procedure's own work, and an early routing error moves a whole subtree. On PARSEME, by contrast, the structure is a set of token groups proposed by rules, and every remaining error is a decision or a missing candidate rule, which is exactly what reflection edits.

\paragraph{Options must follow the annotation, not the inventory.}
The revised \umr procedure completed its inventories from the guidelines, among them the 27 values of the aspect lattice; the training gold uses seven. Given the full lattice, Jev chose values the gold never uses (\emph{directed-achievement} 118 times on H), the judge's aspect score fell from 0.70 for $K_4$ to 0.31, and \F fell to 33.3. The revision also recorded event negation only as modal strength, which the sentence score does not see. Restricting aspect to the seven values and writing negation as \texttt{:polarity -} restored aspect to 0.71 and \F to 38.5, still 0.9 below $J_2$, the version it revised. A decision model answers the question it is asked; whether the options are right is a property of the procedure, which only measurement against gold reveals.

\paragraph{Measurement filters edits; small development sets do not.}
Unmeasured reflection is not filtered: in fragment mode 1 of 441 edits was rejected, and the skills grew from 41k to 156k characters in ten rounds. With measurement, 19 of 48 document-mode and 107 of 190 batch-mode proposals were rejected, among them attempts to undo earlier fixes. Training gains still exceed test gains: in document mode, round 4 raised its development documents by 0.8 \F while H fell from 40.9 to 40.1. In batch mode, the size of the development set appears to matter. With 150 development sentences, PARSEME's development gains carried over to the test sets; on Chinese \umr, the run from $J_0$ with ten development sentences raised development \F by 7.3 but H by only 1.6, and the run from $\Pi_0$ with 27 accepted fewer edits and moved neither score. The seeds differ, so the comparison is suggestive. Repeated trajectories and matched acceptance-policy ablations remain pending \exper{E3}, \exper{E6}, \exper{E8}.

\paragraph{Weak reflectors and cost.}
A 9B reflector breaks anchors and writes noisy rules (new documents score 1.4 \F below the seed). Closed decisions cost \$0.001--0.006 per \umr sentence, against \$0.71 in fragment mode with Sonnet (Appendix~\ref{app:costs}) \exper{E11}.
