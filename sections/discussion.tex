\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Diagnosability}
Each prediction records its step, candidate options and decision scores, so an error points to a particular criterion. In Chinese \umr, the concept checks exposed English names for Chinese words, so that the reflector added a source-word convention to the fragment concept instructions, and test F1 rose from 20.9 to 27.8 in the first round (\S\ref{sec:res-rsi}). In Chinese PARSEME, false light-verb decisions prompted a stricter closed light-verb test, including the rule that \zht{有} is not a light verb; Jev's spurious predictions fell from 80 to 9 over ten rounds. The trace also locates errors reflection has not fixed: 51 of Jev's 212 wrong English event concepts are inflected forms left unchanged by its rule-based lemmatizer (\S\ref{sec:case}). This distinguishes a decision rule that needs revision from code that needs new options.

\paragraph{Low-cost decision models}
For 100 Chinese \umr sentences, parsing costs about \$0.1--0.3 with a decision model, \$1.9 with Haiku, and \$7.1 with GPT-6.1-sol using the full guideline (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:costs}). Cheap closed decisions make it practical to scale. The performance is competitive. Jev gives the best Chinese \umr procedure (37.6 \jm{} F1). The coverage limit is visible in PARSEME: a closed decider cannot recover an expression absent from its candidate list, while fragments can. The transfer result in \S\ref{sec:res-calib} indicates that one revised procedure can serve another decision model, which shows great potential for decision models on this task.

\paragraph{Limitation of reflection}
\label{sec:disc-rsi}
All problems coming together during reflection do not guarantee steady improvement. Fragment edits can drift such as Haiku's Chinese PARSEME score peaks at 62.9 at $V_3$ and ends at 53.2. Even measured closed edits are selected on small batches, and recalibrating their thresholds on all 50 training sentences can lower test F1. Reflection revises rules within the existing decomposition determined by codes, and it cannot add candidates that the closed PARSEME enumerator never proposes without a code change, which may cause problems. It also does not close the gap to the one-shot model on English PARSEME, where the task fits the frontier model's prior knowledge.
