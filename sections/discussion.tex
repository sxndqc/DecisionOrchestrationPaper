\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Diagnosability.}
A guideline in context fails silently: GPT-6.1-sol's Chinese \umr graphs are worse than our seeds', and nothing in its output says which of the 366k characters it misread. Each error of a procedure has an address, a step, its input, its options and their probabilities, and that changes how errors are found and fixed. The Haiku seed's English concepts for Chinese words, Jev's unlemmatized English verbs and the closed PARSEME seeds' flood of spurious light-verb constructions (\S\ref{sec:case}) were each found from one column of the judge's output and fixed by one edit to one file: by the reflector for the first and the third, and the second is a one-line change to the code that lists the options. The same address makes reflection possible at all: the reflector reads the decisions behind each difference, not just the difference, and its edits are diffs that an annotator can review, keep or revert. We see this as the main practical argument for the approach early in a project, when people and models correct each other.

\paragraph{Decision models are cheap and promising.}
Decision models never write text: they return probabilities over options that code has listed. This makes them the cheapest deciders we tried. Annotating the 100 Chinese \umr test sentences costs \$0.28 with Jev and \$0.19 with Microsoft-Decision-1, against \$1.88 with Haiku in fragment mode and \$7.09 for GPT-6.1-sol with the guideline; the 100 PARSEME sentences cost about one cent with either service. A closed parse is fast enough that every edit of reflection can be measured, which is what keeps closed-mode reflection from drifting. And the closed procedure is, as of now, the more accurate one on Chinese \umr (Jev 37.6) and close on English (31.8 against 32.6). Its ceiling is set by the code around it: a decision model cannot pick an option that is not offered (the lemmatizer, \S\ref{sec:case}; PARSEME expressions no rule proposes) and cannot write structure. Better enumerators, which are ordinary code, raise that ceiling for every decision model at once, and the transfer result (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}) suggests that a procedure learned with one decision model can be handed to another.

\paragraph{What reflection can and cannot do.}
\label{sec:disc-rsi}
With five gold sentences a round, reflection writes the conventions a guideline leaves implicit (Chinese concepts stay Chinese; \zht{有} is not a light verb; \zh{但} is \texttt{but-91}) and fixes procedural errors (a missing candidate kind, a YAML convention). It does so from very little data: the largest gains come in the first rounds (Haiku Chinese \umr $+6.9$ in round 1; Jev Chinese PARSEME $+20.1$ in rounds 3--4). Three limits are visible. (1) \emph{Unmeasured edits drift.} In fragment mode we accept every edit that applies, and Haiku's Chinese PARSEME procedure lost almost ten points between rounds 3 and 10 although each edit was locally sensible; measured acceptance gives the closed chains curves that rarely go down. Measuring fragment edits costs a re-parse per edit, which a cheap fragment decider would make affordable. (2) \emph{The training batches are tiny.} Closed edits are accepted on changes of a few points on five sentences, and the gap between training and test grows over the rounds (Jev Chinese PARSEME: 88.2 on training batches, 63.4 on test); threshold calibration on the same 50 sentences overfits (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}). More gold makes both checks stronger, and the procedure keeps improving as gold arrives. (3) \emph{Reflection edits what exists.} It adds rules, options and questions, but in ten rounds it rarely changed the decomposition itself (which step decides what); in English PARSEME, where the frontier model with the guideline reaches 75.6, the procedures' gains flatten well below it.

\paragraph{When to decompose.}
The frontier model in context is strongest where the scheme is close to what such models already know and the output is a short list, English verbal MWEs; decomposition is strongest where the output is structure and the conventions are particular, Chinese \umr. For a new scheme or language early in a project, the procedure costs a few dollars to run, improves from the first batches of gold, and leaves a trace in which every error can be found.
